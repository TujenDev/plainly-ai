The rest of the site

Three other places worth knowing about, since they answer different kinds of question:

  • Start here places you in one of three tracks and gives a sequenced reading list for it. The right first stop if you don't know where you are.
  • Concepts holds the ten explainers: what a token is, why models invent things, how tool use works, and so on. Read those when you want to understand rather than act.
  • Model facts carries the per-model numbers, deliberately kept to one page so a single page rots instead of a hundred.
  • Sources lists every outside document the site depends on, what it is cited for, and when it was last read. The maintenance list, published.

What decides whether something is a guide

A concept page explains how a thing works. A guide tells you what to do about it. Keeping the two apart is what lets the concept pages avoid practical advice that dates, and lets the guides stay short by pointing at the concepts instead of re-explaining them.
