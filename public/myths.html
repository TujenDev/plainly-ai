Guide · A router, not an argument

Common myths, and where each one is answered

The beliefs that come up most, and the page that deals with each. Nothing is argued here: every line is a link to an explanation that carries its own sources. The reason for that restraint is the first section.

Published 30 August 2026  ·  Last verified 30 August 2026  ·  A router. Every claim it points at is dated on the page that makes it

Why this page argues nothing#

Every line below is a belief people arrive with. None of them is argued against here — each one points at the page that explains the mechanism underneath it, and the explanation lives there.

That is a deliberate constraint rather than a stylistic one. This site's page on whether what you're reading is out of date makes the case that a confident claim about what these systems “can't do” has the shortest shelf life of any sentence in the field. A page built out of such sentences would therefore be the fastest rotting page here, and it would rot invisibly: a myth-busting list reads as authoritative long after the thing it busted stopped being true.

The rule this page runs on

A line only goes on this list if a page here already explains the mechanism, and the line links to it. Nothing is asserted on this page that is not argued somewhere else with its sources attached. If a belief is worth correcting and no page explains why, the work is to write that page, not to add a sentence here. That is what stops a router turning into a place where claims quietly accumulate.
